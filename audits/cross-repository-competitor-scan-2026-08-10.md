## Decision summary

This is competitive validation, not customer validation. Shipped products and public prices prove that buyers fund these categories. They do not prove demand for these products, so this report earns no market-validation points by itself.

| Priority | Product | Honest decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Locus** | Continue only through outcome-bound validation |
| 2 | **Gatekit + Agent Access** | Combine into one action-time trust stack |
| 3 | **Cortex** | Verticalize around one audited operational workflow |
| 4 | **SceneGuard** | Pilot one non-human change event before more platform work |
| 5 | **Studentlytics** | Pivot away from attendance surveillance or pause |

## 1. Locus

**Demand and overlap.** Coding-agent spend is real: Cursor charges $40 per user per month for Teams and sells an Enterprise tier with repository controls, audit logs, and service accounts. Its Agent already searches the codebase, reads files, calls tools, and automatically selects relevant code and session context ([Cursor pricing](https://cursor.com/pricing), [Cursor context](https://docs.cursor.com/en/guides/working-with-context)). Context retrieval is therefore a bundled capability, not an uncontested category.

**Surviving wedge.** Locus can be the vendor-neutral evidence layer across agents: deterministic task-to-Slice admission, explicit whole-Repo widening, visible included and excluded paths, and total-token measurement bound to a human-accepted outcome. The buyer is an AI platform or engineering-infrastructure team using multiple agents. The claim must be "equal outcome quality at lower total cost or with better reviewability," not "smaller prompt."

**Why it may fail.** Integrated agents can copy visible context and isolated exploration; larger context windows weaken token-saving value; current language and packaging limits narrow adoption; historical fix-file recall does not prove agent outcomes.

**Next proof:** Complete the paired task study and run three design-partner comparisons against incumbent agents on real accepted changes.

## 2. Gatekit

**Demand and overlap.** Amazon AgentCore Policy checks each Gateway tool action and charges $0.000025 per authorization request. Permit exposes an MCP gateway, policy engine, approvals, delegation, decision logs, audit retention, GitOps, and enterprise deployment, with paid entry tiers from $5 and $25 per month ([AgentCore pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/pricing/), [Permit pricing](https://www.permit.io/pricing)). Generic pre-action policy, approval, and audit are already shipped.

**Surviving wedge.** Build a framework-neutral enforcement point that normalizes the proposed action, binds it to delegated authority and an exact policy version, blocks execution until deterministic policy or human approval succeeds, and emits an independently verifiable decision receipt. It must work across self-hosted agents and clouds without taking over the buyer's identity or runtime.

**Why it may fail.** Existing cloud, IAM, and API-gateway vendors own distribution. Another gateway adds latency and availability risk. Approval without identity-bound credential release cannot ensure that only the approved action executes.

**Portfolio decision:** Combine with Agent Access. The sellable unit is principal and delegation -> policy decision -> scoped credential -> execution -> signed receipt.

## 3. Cortex

**Demand and overlap.** Guru already sells permission-aware cited answers, automatic verification across connected sources, lineage, audit logs, and MCP delivery. GitBook can turn a documentation URL into a multi-file `SKILL.md` package compatible with more than 30 agents ([Guru pricing](https://www.getguru.com/pricing), [GitBook Skill Generator](https://gitbook.com/skill-generator)). Cited knowledge and skill export are features in mature platforms.

**Surviving wedge.** Do not build generic enterprise search or a skill generator. Build a release process for one high-consequence operating domain: every executable skill version comes from approved source revisions, has sentence-level citations, shows a semantic diff, passes domain tests, records approvers, and can be rolled back. Good candidates are compliance operations or incident response, where stale instructions have a measurable cost.

**Why it may fail.** Incumbents own connectors, permissions, freshness, and distribution. Exported skills can become stale immediately. Without one regulated workflow and measurable reduction in errors or update time, Cortex is a feature.

**Next proof:** One design partner must supply an audited runbook, current source documents, and a paid pilot measuring error rate, time-to-update, and evidence completeness.

## 4. Agent Access

**Demand and overlap.** Amazon AgentCore Identity ships workload identities, token vaults, user-bound access, credential-provider scoping, and audit integration. Identity is included with its Runtime or Gateway and otherwise costs $0.010 per thousand external token or API-key requests. Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol already uses HTTP Message Signatures so a merchant can verify an initially unknown approved agent, linked consumer identity, and commerce intent ([AgentCore pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/pricing/), [Visa specification](https://developer.visa.com/capabilities/trusted-agent-protocol/trusted-agent-protocol-specifications/)). Credential brokerage and signed agent intent are both being standardized by large incumbents.

**Surviving wedge.** Focus on cross-organization trust for external agents: accept delegation from an existing IdP, issue least-privilege short-lived access, preserve attenuation through multi-agent delegation, and give the downstream relying party a receipt it can verify without trusting the agent operator. Bind principal, agent, normalized action, constraints, policy version, credential release, result hash, and revocation state.

**Why it may fail.** Identity buyers consolidate around their existing cloud or IdP. A receipt has no value until relying parties trust its issuer and verify it. Standards and payment networks can absorb the protocol. Separating this from Gatekit weakens both products.

**Next proof:** A third-party agent must perform one constrained action in a buyer-owned system, with the buyer independently verifying the delegation and receipt. A self-verified demo does not count.

## 5. SceneGuard

**Demand and overlap.** Density sells anonymous radar sensors for $229 plus software from $8 per room per month. Ubiquiti performs detection, search, alerts, and incident reporting entirely on premises, with locally stored video and AI processing ([Density](https://density.io/), [UniFi privacy](https://help.ui.com/hc/en-us/articles/31234972188951-How-UniFi-Protect-Protects-Your-Data)). Privacy-first spatial analytics has budget, but both camera-free hardware and complete local video systems already exist.

**Surviving wedge.** Avoid security, occupancy, and people analytics. Use existing fixed cameras to detect one non-human state change while processing locally and discarding frames: obstructed exits, moved equipment, altered lab stations, or shelf depletion. Sell a completed operational event and workflow integration, not computer vision.

**Why it may fail.** Camera-free sensors have a stronger privacy claim. NVRs bundle local inference and evidence. Deleting imagery makes false positives hard to investigate. Lighting, occlusion, camera movement, and site calibration can destroy software-only economics.

**Next proof:** A facilities owner must name one costly event and run a 30-day pilot with declared miss and false-alarm thresholds. Do not add identity or emotion inference.

## 6. Studentlytics

**Demand and overlap.** Panopto tracks individual watch time, completion, pauses, rewinds, drop-off, and quiz comprehension. EchoVideo calculates attendance from classroom or recording interaction and lets instructors weight video, polling, questions, notes, and other activity into an engagement score ([Panopto analytics](https://www.panopto.com/capabilities/tracking-and-analytics/), [EchoVideo metrics](https://support.echo360.com/hc/en-us/articles/11077110560653-EchoVideo-Definitions-of-Course-Analytics-Metrics)). Attendance and engagement are already bundled with institutional video platforms.

**Surviving wedge.** Pivot to recording-agnostic, institution-hosted, aggregate teaching diagnostics: confusing segments, setup time, accessibility gaps, topic-to-assessment gaps, and suggested edits. Use authenticated platform events for attendance. Never infer attention, emotion, or learning from faces.

**Why it may fail.** Institutions already own capture, LMS, polling, gradebook, and analytics workflows. Watch behavior is a weak proxy for comprehension. Student-linked video analytics increase governance burden, and EU law prohibits emotion recognition in education except for medical or safety reasons ([European Commission summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A52025SC0368)).

**Next proof:** Continue only if two teaching and learning leaders who already own a video platform provide de-identified recordings and pay for a specific teaching-quality decision their current dashboard cannot support.

## Portfolio action

1. Finish Locus outcome validation before adding breadth.
2. Merge Gatekit and Agent Access positioning and customer discovery.
3. Freeze horizontal work on Cortex, SceneGuard, and Studentlytics until each has a narrow buyer, a current artifact or dataset, and a committed pilot.

The next honest score increase requires external buyer behavior: a completed interview plus a current artifact, followed by a pilot, payment, or production use.
